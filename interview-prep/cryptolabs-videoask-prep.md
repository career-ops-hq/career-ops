## Q1 - CONFIRMED: "If you had 100K of capital to deploy and your primary goal was cash flow, how would you structure a portfolio to help you meet that goal?"

*Spine of the answer: reframe "cash flow" = durable real yield -> risk-tier the capital -> drill the "where does the yield come from" question -> close on smart-contract risk. ~100-120s. Trim the last paragraph if running long.*

Good question. First thing I'd do is reframe it: "cash flow" means durable, recurring yield - so the job isn't chasing the highest APY on the screen, it's finding yield I actually understand and trust to still be there next month.

So I'd think in risk tiers. Roughly half - call it 50K - is the ballast: stablecoins earning real yield. Lending on battle-tested money markets, plus some yield backed by tokenized treasuries. Boring, predictable, mid-single-digit returns - and it's boring on purpose.

Next tier, about 25K, into liquid staking on major assets like Ethereum and Solana. That yield comes straight from the network, so it's about as real as crypto yield gets, and I keep exposure to the assets themselves.

Then maybe 20K into providing liquidity - but only in pools where I'm earning actual trading fees, not token emissions. Usually stablecoin pairs, where I'm not fighting impermanent loss. And I'd keep the last 5K in stablecoins as dry powder.

The thread through all of it is one question I'd drill into every member: where is this yield actually coming from? If it traces back to fees, lending, or staking - good. If it's just a protocol printing its own token to pay you, that's not cash flow, that's a countdown.

And the thing I'd never let a beginner forget: in DeFi the real risk isn't just price, it's the smart contract. So I diversify across protocols and stick to audited, proven ones - because a 12% yield means nothing if the contract gets drained.

## Q2 - CONFIRMED: "Could you explain your project research process? How do you find new pairs, new pools, and new opportunities? Which metrics are you looking at?"

*Structure: funnel - discover wide -> filter on real usage -> DD the risk. Name metrics explicitly (they asked). Contract-level risk is your finale - it's the edge nobody else has. Group the metrics so it flows, don't rattle them robotically. ~110-130s; trim if long.*

I treat it like a funnel - discover wide, then filter hard.

For discovery, I start with the on-chain dashboards - DeFiLlama for TVL and yields, Dune for the deeper cuts - and I follow builders, not influencers. The best early signal for a new pool is usually someone credible quietly using it before it's being shilled.

Then the filtering, and this is where the metrics come in. First, TVL - but the trend more than the number: is it growing, and is it sticky, or is it mercenary capital that leaves the moment rewards drop? Then volume and fees relative to TVL, because fees are the honest signal that a pool is actually being used, not just farmed. Then I break down the APY: how much is real yield from fees versus the protocol just printing its own token. A 40% APY that's 90% emissions is a completely different animal from 8% that's all fees. And for a specific pair, I check how correlated the two assets are - that's my impermanent-loss risk - and how deep the liquidity is, because that tells me whether I can actually exit.

But the layer most people skip is the one I go to first, because of my background: I read the contract. Is it audited, and by whom? Is it a fork of something proven, or brand-new code? Who controls the upgrade keys - a multisig, or one anonymous dev who can drain it? How long has it survived on Mainnet? Because the highest APY on the screen is worthless if the risk underneath it is a single admin key.

So the final question is always the same: is this yield real, is it durable, and does it justify the risk I just mapped? If yes, I size in carefully. If I can't answer where the yield comes from, I pass.

## Q3 - CONFIRMED: "How would you deal with someone losing money... maybe they got hacked... or it was a user error... or maybe they lost their money to a rug pull or a scam?"

*THIS IS THE EMPATHY QUESTION - not a technical one. Lead with the human, tech comes second. Tone shift: warmer, slower, softer than Q1/Q2. Order: empathy -> triage by case -> honest about scams + recovery-scam warning -> gentle lesson -> community-trust close. ~120-130s.*

First thing - and this matters more than any technical advice - I'd meet them with empathy, not a lecture. Someone who just lost money is scared and probably embarrassed, and the last thing they need is "well, you should have checked." Almost everyone in crypto has lost money at some point. I have. So I'd start by taking the shame out of it.

Then I'd get practical and figure out which situation we're in, because they're different. If they were hacked or made a mistake but still have funds at risk - a wallet with bad approvals, say - the priority is stopping the bleeding: revoke the approvals, move whatever's left to a fresh wallet, lock it down. That's the moment to move fast.

If it was a rug pull or an outright scam, I'd be honest with them: in most of those cases the money is gone, and chasing it usually makes it worse. I'd specifically warn them about recovery scammers - the people who slide into your DMs promising to get your funds back for a fee. That's a second scam preying on the first, and victims fall for it because they're desperate. Protecting them from that is part of the job.

Then, once they've had a moment, I'd turn it into a lesson - gently. What actually happened, and what one habit prevents it next time: revoking approvals regularly, never signing a transaction you don't understand, keeping serious money in a hardware wallet, verifying a contract before you touch it. The goal isn't to make them feel stupid. It's to make sure they pay the tuition once and never again.

Because honestly, how a community treats someone on their worst day is what earns real trust. Anyone can celebrate the wins.

## Q4 - CONFIRMED (VIDEO ONLY): "Can you share an example of a common mistake that new DeFi investors make, and how you would coach them through it?"

*FINAL / last impression. Pick a FRESH mistake not used in Q1-Q3: position sizing / FOMO (behavioral, not technical). Demonstrate coaching STYLE: no scolding -> one Socratic question -> a concrete rule -> "keep them in the game" philosophy. End strong and warm. ~120-130s.*

The most common one I see isn't technical - it's position sizing. A beginner sees someone post a 10x on Twitter, feels like they're missing out, and puts way too much into a single play - often money they can't actually afford to lose. And then every price wiggle becomes an emotional event, because their rent is riding on it.

So I wouldn't coach that by scolding them - that just teaches people to hide their mistakes from you. I'd start by separating the decision from the emotion, with one simple question: "if this position went to zero tomorrow, does it change your life?" If the answer is yes, it's too big - full stop. And that reframes the whole thing from "how much could I make" to "how much can I afford to lose."

Then I'd give them a concrete habit, because willpower doesn't work but rules do. Something like: no single position is more than a set slice of your portfolio, keep a stablecoin base you never touch, and take a little profit on the way up so you're playing with house money. Simple rules they can follow even when they're emotional.

And I'd be honest about what my job is. I'm not here to hand anyone the next hot token - anyone promising you that is selling you something. My job is to keep them in the game long enough to actually get good. Because the people who win in this space aren't the ones who caught one big pump - they're the ones who never blew up. Protecting the downside is the whole skill.
