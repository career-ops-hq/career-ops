🚀 Want to thrive at a tech startup? There is one skill that compounds your value there - and most people don't even list it on their CV 🤫

It's the ability to become useful, fast, in a stack you've never shipped before. Most engineers underrate it - they're just used to listing programming languages, frameworks, design patterns, software engineering principles, that kind of stuff.

At a tech startup, the products you start working on when you join are rarely the same ones you're shipping six months later. New tools land, priorities shift, the road map gets rewritten - and in the era of AI, things move even faster. That's the environment where adaptable engineers compound their value.

My own path spans three domains: automotive (embedded C/C++), civil engineering (desktop C#), and now blockchain - starting with Solidity Smart Contracts and audits, then a REVM fork in Rust + co-authoring EIP-7809, and currently Anchor programs live on Solana mainnet.

The first few weeks in any new stack are uncomfortable, sometimes outright painful. But then the puzzle pieces start clicking into a bigger picture - and there's a real sense of accomplishment in shipping something solid in a domain you barely knew weeks earlier, after all the brainstorming and pain it took to get there. ⏳
